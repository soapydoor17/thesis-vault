# Formatting

## Structure of Pages
- 12pt Times New Roman (or equivalent)
- 15-20 pages long (i.e., Title, Table of Contents, Abstract, Nomenclature (optional), Aims, References, Appendix (optional), and between 10 and 15 text pages) 
- Written single spaced
- Justified across the page, i.e., not left aligned. 
- Under no circumstances should the Progress Report be more than 20 pages in length unless you have received prior permission from your Project Supervisor. 
- The report should be written on A4 sized paper with margins of 2.5 cm (top, right, and bottom) and 4.0 cm (left).
- Individual paragraphs should be delineated from their neighbours by either: (i) a half (6 point) or single (12 point) blank line with no indent on the first line of the new paragraph or (ii) no blank line between neighbouring paragraphs but with each new paragraph being indented by a single tab character. In either case, the first paragraph below a section heading should not be indented.
- Each page apart from the Title Page should contain a page number as part of the page footer. The page number should be centre aligned and 12 point Times New Roman text with the format of the page number being either: (i) Page X, (ii) X, or (iii) - X - (note the spaces), where X is the page number. Do not include any other header or footer information or ornaments such as student details, project details, or horizontal ruled lines.

## Writing
- The report should be written in the past impersonal tense and avoid using the words “I” and “we”. The assumption of the reader will be that, unless otherwise stated, you were responsible for everything carried out within the semester and thus you do not need to continually state “I did” something. If you must refer to yourself then use “the author” instead of “I”.
- All symbols should be in italics and their meaning explained (e.g., the strength, S, of the specimen). Standard non-English phrases and abbreviations such as i.e., e.g., etc., in situ, and in vivo should be italicised. If you wish to emphasise or make a particular point within the text then use italics rather than bold or underlining.

## Figures, Equations and Tables
- Each figure or table in the laboratory report should be continuously numbered (e.g., Figure 1) and possess a caption using 12 point Times New Roman Italic (or similar) font and, if necessary, a reference.
- Figure captions should be placed below the figure and separated by a blank line
- Table captions should be placed above the table and separated by a blank line. 
- The axes of figures should be correctly labelled and units indicated where necessary. 
- Reference to a figure, table, equation, or section within the text should be capitalised, e.g., “as shown in Figure 1 we can see that” or “as mentioned previously in the Results and Discussion section”.
- Equations should be computer generated and separated from text by a one and a half (18 point) blank line and indented by one tab character (10~15 mm) from the left margin. Equations should be numbered sequentially with the numbers being positioned adjacent to the right edge of the margin and enclosed in brackets.

## Referencing
- You may use any standard referencing system agreed upon by yourself and your Project Supervisor. If your Project Supervisor has no preference then you should use the Chicago referencing system, e.g., (Tanaka, 2007) {Single Author} or (Tanaka & Itatani, 2007) {Two Authors} or (Tanaka et al., 2007) {Three or more Authors} as appropriate. When the references are listed at the back of your report, the “et al.” in the final example should be replaced by the full list of authors.
