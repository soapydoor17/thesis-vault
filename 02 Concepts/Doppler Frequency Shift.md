## Definition
The Doppler effect is an alteration in the observed frequency of a sound due to motion of either the source or the observer. Although less familiar, this effect is easily noticed for a stationary source and moving observer. For example, if you ride a train past a stationary warning horn, you will hear the horn’s frequency shift from high to low as you pass by. The actual change in frequency due to relative motion of source and observer is called a Doppler shift.

---

In this case, the moving source is the satellite (moving roughly 7-8km/s?) and the stationary observer is the Binar ground station

CLAUDE - As the satellite rises above the horizon and approaches the ground station, it's closing the distance between them — so the radio waves get compressed and you receive a _higher_ frequency than the satellite is actually transmitting. As it passes overhead and starts moving away, the distance is increasing — waves get stretched and you receive a _lower_ frequency. Right at the moment of closest approach (highest elevation angle), the frequency briefly matches the true transmitted frequency because the satellite is moving perpendicular to your line of sight, neither towards nor away.

The frequency shift can be used to find the [[Range Rate]]:
- $f_\text{transmitted} = f_0$
- $c =$ speed of light
$$
\Delta f = f_\text{recieved} - f_\text{transmitted}
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta f}{f_\text{transmitted}} = \frac{-\text{range rate}}{c}
$$
