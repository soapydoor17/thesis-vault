## Definition
J2 is the coefficient of the second-degree term in the spherical harmonic expansion of Earth's gravitational potential, representing the dominant deviation from a perfectly spherical mass distribution
i.e., Earth's equatorial bulge. For Earth, J2 is roughly 1.08×10⁻³, an order of magnitude larger than any other harmonic coefficient, making it by far the largest orbital perturbation for satellites at typical altitudes

Earth is like a round ball, but if you're pressing down on it from the top and bottom -> bulges out at the equator and poles are flatter. Gravity is a tiny bit stronger around the bulge and pulls in a slightly different direction than a perfectly round ball. The J2 coefficient describes the behaviour of gravity in this situation. J3 , J4 etc. describe further imperfections, but aren't as important as J2.
J0 would be a perfectly round ball and J1 would be like a lop-sided blob (but scientists measure from Earth's exact centre of mass, so this turns out to be equivalent to J1 I believe?)
